# Synthetic Customer Simulator

## Purpose

Provide founders and developers with immediate pre-build validation by simulating realistic customer segments with varying price sensitivity, switching barriers, domain cynicism, and daily habits so they know if the project is genuinely viable before starting.

## Activate when

At the start of project discovery, before starting development, when a new product idea needs market validation, or when testing pricing and positioning models. Select this capability only when it applies to the request.

## Inputs

- project concept or objective
- target customer profiles
- proposed pricing model
- competitor landscape
- core value proposition statement

## Procedure

1. Define 5-10 distinct customer archetypes matching the product domain (e.g., budget-conscious student, busy freelancer, enterprise manager, tech-savvy early adopter, cynical non-technical user) and scale them into a representative 100-persona cohort.
2. Assign explicit parameters to each persona: budget constraint, willingness to pay, current alternative tool, switching friction tolerance, and primary daily frustration.
3. Pitch the project's exact core value proposition and proposed pricing to all 100 simulated personas in parallel.
4. Tally initial buy/reject decisions and calculate baseline conversion (e.g., 14/100 purchase rate). Extract precise rejection rationales from the non-buyers.
5. Analyze pricing sensitivity: simulate reactions at 50%, 100%, and 150% of the target price to determine the revenue-maximizing price point.
6. Identify the top 3 product and positioning bottlenecks (e.g., confusing value metric, lack of integration with existing stack, perceived high switching cost).
7. Formulate 3 concrete pivots/remedies (repackaging, tiering, or narrowing to a specific niche) and re-test the cohort to observe delta in conversion.
8. Issue a final project feasibility verdict: 'GREEN / BUILD' (conversion > 40%), 'AMBER / PIVOT' (requires proposition adjustment), or 'RED / DO NOT START' (fundamental lack of demand or willingness to pay).

## Avoid

- Assuming all synthetic personas are enthusiastic tech early adopters who love new tools.
- Allowing personas to agree without expressing realistic objections regarding cost, habit, and friction.
- Treating synthetic simulation as a substitute for real customer interviews — use it to eliminate obvious non-starters before speaking to humans.
- Masking a 'RED / DO NOT START' result with vague optimistic advice.

## Expected outputs

- customer persona distribution breakdown
- simulated purchase conversion rate
- price elasticity curve
- top objections and friction points
- unfiltered customer feedback log
- definitive Build vs Kill/Pivot verdict

## Validation

- persona cohort contains realistic cynics and budget-constrained users
- conversion rate backed by itemized persona decisions
- rejection rationales explicitly recorded
- definitive Build vs Pivot vs Kill recommendation provided
- Report checks that were not run as unverified.
- Provide evidence references and actionable remediation for failures.
